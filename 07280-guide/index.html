Intro to AI & ML, taught properly

Every concept from the course slides and notes — rebuilt from the ground up with intuition, analogies, full derivations, worked examples, interactive figures and guided problems.

Why this exists

The slides state results. They rarely teach them: a definition appears, a symbol is introduced without being named, a proof step says TODO, a poll asks a question and the answer lives in a clicker app you can't go back to.

This guide works the other way round. Every idea starts from something concrete, gets an analogy, then a definition with every symbol spelled out, then a numeric example computed all the way through, then the full derivation, then problems you solve yourself with the steps hidden until you want them. Nothing is left as an exercise to the reader.

The shape of the course

Two halves that look unrelated and aren't. Search asks how do I find a good answer in a space too large to enumerate? Machine learning asks exactly the same question — the space is the set of hypotheses, and gradient descent is the search algorithm.

One honest caveat

Several figures in the source PDFs are images that don't survive text extraction. Where a chapter needed one of those — the Iris comparison tables, the expectimax numbers, the overfitting panels — it has been reconstructed so the idea still lands, and the page says so at the point of use. Check those specific figures against your own copy of the slides before you rely on exact numbers.
